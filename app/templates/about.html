About MATRIXCMS

The official expedition data management, sensor telemetry archive, and automated scientific communication repository of the Department of Computer Science & Engineering, Chandigarh.

1. Institutional Context & Governance

MATRIXCMS was conceived and developed within the Department of Computer Science & Engineering at Chandigarh to bridge empirical environmental engineering with computational data infrastructure. Academic field campaigns frequently gather crucial environmental, atmospheric, and geophysical observations in challenging high-altitude or riverine terrains; MATRIXCMS serves as the permanent digital repository for this scholarly output.

The portal is maintained under the direct auspices of the Department’s Computational Sensing and Environmental Informatics Research Group, providing custody, digital preservation, and public dissemination of primary scientific materials.

2. Portal Mission & Functional Pillars

Open Science & Reproducibility

MATRIXCMS guarantees unrestricted public access to raw and calibrated observational data, software processing notebooks, and technical instrumentation logs. We ensure that empirical findings can be independently inspected, replicated, and cited by international research institutions.

Longitudinal Expedition Archival

Every scientific campaign—ranging from high-altitude permafrost acoustic mesh deployments to regional atmospheric aerosol transects—is documented with rigorous provenance: team leadership, spatial coordinates, calendar windows, instruments, and linked publications.

Automated Outreach via Free LLMs

To bridge technical field reports and civic comprehension, the portal integrates zero-cost, high-performance open-weight language model backends (including Groq API and Cloudflare Workers AI running Meta Llama 3.1 8B Instruct and Llama 3.2). The system autonomously drafts accessible web summaries, scientific abstracts, and outreach bulletins.

Human-in-the-Loop Safeguard: Every automated outreach artifact is staged in a strict pending_review state and requires manual validation by faculty data managers prior to public indexation.

3. Architecture & Metadata Standards

To ensure enduring scholarly interoperability, MATRIXCMS adheres to international scientific metadata conventions:

FAIR Principles
Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, and Reusable: All datasets incorporate rich structured descriptive metadata, persistent identifiers, standardized parameter naming conventions, and open machine-readable formats (CSV, JSON, NetCDF, GeoTIFF).
DOI Registration
Persistent Digital Object Identifiers: Datasets and technical monographs are prepared for permanent DOI minting via DataCite and Zenodo integrations, guaranteeing stable citations across international journals and bibliometric indices.
CC-BY-4.0
Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International: All published observational data, photographic evidence, and public reports are licensed under CC-BY-4.0, granting worldwide permissions to share, adapt, and build upon our work with proper attribution.
Dublin Core & ISO 19115
Geospatial & Bibliographic Schema: Field observations conform to Dublin Core Element Sets for document classification and ISO 19115 geographic information metadata for spatial coordinates and bounding polygons.

4. Technical Specifications

Core Web Framework FastAPI (Asynchronous Python ASGI Web Architecture)
Templating Engine Jinja2 with clean server-side rendering and semantic HTML5
Relational ORM SQLAlchemy 2.0 with Alembic schema migration framework
Database Engine SQLite (Development/Edge) / PostgreSQL (Production)
Automated LLM Engine Groq Cloud & Cloudflare Workers AI (Meta Llama 3.1 8B / 70B & Llama 3.2 Instruct)
Task Scheduling APScheduler for periodic content generation and sensor health polling
Authentication JWT (JSON Web Tokens) with Argon2/BCrypt hashing and RBAC enforcement
